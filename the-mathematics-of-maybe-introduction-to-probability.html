Formula Sheet

1. Theoretical Probability

Probability of an event E = Number of favourable outcomes ÷ Total number of equally likely outcomes

P(E) = n(E) / n(S)

2. Experimental Probability

Experimental probability = Number of times the event occurs ÷ Total number of trials

3. Relative Frequency

Relative frequency = Frequency of an event ÷ Total observations

4. Estimating a Population

Estimated number = Probability estimated from the sample × Total population

5. Probability Range

0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
Impossible event: P(E) = 0
Certain event: P(E) = 1

6. Complementary Events

P(not E) = 1 − P(E)

7. Equally Likely Outcomes

If all outcomes have the same chance, probability is the number of favourable outcomes divided by the total number of outcomes.

8. Combined Independent Events

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)
Use this when the two events are independent.

9. Law of Large Numbers

As the number of trials increases, experimental probability tends to get closer to theoretical probability.
Symbols: P(E) means the probability of event E. n(E) is the number of favourable outcomes, and n(S) is the total number of outcomes in the sample space.

Prime sums greater than 5 are 7 and 11.

P = (6 + 2)/36 = 2/9

Solution 16
Rectangle dimensions = 3 m × 2 m.

Area of rectangle = 3 × 2 = 6 m².

Circle diameter = 1 m, so radius = 1/2 m.

Area of circle = πr² = π × (1/2)² = π/4 m².

Assuming the point is equally likely to land anywhere in the rectangle:

Probability = Area of circle ÷ Area of rectangle
= (π/4) ÷ 6
Probability = π/24 ≈ 0.131

Quick Revision

What is a sample space?
A sample space is the set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment.
What is an event?
An event is an outcome or a collection of outcomes from a sample space.
Can probability be negative or greater than 1?
No. Every probability lies between 0 and 1, including both endpoints.
Does a coin landing heads repeatedly guarantee tails next?
No. For a fair coin, every independent toss still has a probability of 1/2 for heads and 1/2 for tails.
